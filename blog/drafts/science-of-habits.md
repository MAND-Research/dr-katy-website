# The Science of Habits

*Original post from April 13, 2023*

---

With the start of the new year, many people contemplate their goals and aspirations, hoping to finally abandon old patterns and establish new ones that support their objectives.

However, by February or March, a significant portion of people will have abandoned their efforts. Why do beneficial habits prove so challenging to establish? Why do harmful habits resist elimination?

## Understanding Habits

Habits represent a neurological efficiency mechanism—a mental shortcut. When activities are performed repeatedly, they require minimal conscious thought. Imagine needing to deliberate about each small daily task like dressing or brushing teeth; the cognitive burden would prove overwhelming.

Habits are essentially a neurological preservation technique—a shortcut for our brains. This conservation of mental energy permits focus on complex reasoning and important decisions rather than mundane tasks.

## Why Bad Habits Form Easily

Many substances and behaviors labeled "bad"—including substance use, sugar consumption, gambling, and internet browsing—activate the brain's reward system. Although long-term consequences are negative, immediate gratification perpetuates the cycle.

Conversely, beneficial habits prove difficult to develop because they do not strongly trigger reward mechanisms. Individuals must endure less-pleasurable short-term experiences to achieve long-term wellness gains.

## The Path Forward

"Only things you do for a long time positively impact your life expectancy," according to Dan Buettner, author of *The Blue Zones Solution*. Research indicates habit formation requires anywhere from 18 to 254 days.

Two effective approaches:

**Start Small:** Add or remove one habit monthly. After assessment, consider incorporating another change.

**Go Big:** Implement multiple habit changes simultaneously, potentially providing faster results and sustained motivation—provided goals remain realistic.
