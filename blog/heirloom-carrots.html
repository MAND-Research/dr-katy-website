Heirloom Carrots: Yum!

If you've seen heirloom carrots at a farmer's market or grocery store, you may have had mixed feelings. They look like carrots but they're purple or yellow or magenta, sometimes even almost black. Sometimes an unfamiliar vegetable can make us feel a little uncomfortable.

Origin and History

Heirloom varieties emerged before industrial food production became dominant. Small-scale farmers historically selected seeds from their best-tasting vegetables to plant the following year. Over generations, this selective breeding created diverse variations. The term "heirloom" now denotes these older, more interesting vegetable varieties.

Taste and Preparation

Heirloom carrots taste similar to standard carrots but with slightly enhanced flavor. They work in any recipe calling for regular carrots. Since cooking diminishes their distinctive colors, raw preparations best showcase their visual appeal. Carrots don't need to be peeled as long as they are cleaned well with water. Softened refrigerated carrots can be revived by submerging them in water for approximately half a day.

Recipe: Simple Summer Heirloom Carrot Salad

Ingredients:

  • 2 cups heirloom carrots, washed and grated
  • Lemon juice to taste
  • 1 tablespoon extra virgin olive oil
  • Dash sea salt or kosher salt

Instructions: Combine ingredients and serve. The recipe easily accommodates modifications like fresh herbs, seeds, nuts, or dried fruit.
