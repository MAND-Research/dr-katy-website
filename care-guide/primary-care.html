Primary Care and Integrative Medicine

One of the most common questions I get is whether I can be someone's primary care provider. The short answer is: I provide integrative care that's meant to complement primary care, not replace it.

Here's what that means in practice and why I think both relationships matter.

What a primary care provider does

Your PCP is your medical home base. They handle:

  • Annual physicals and preventive screenings
  • Acute illness (infections, injuries, sudden symptoms)
  • Referrals to specialists
  • Prescription medications when needed
  • Urgent concerns that need same-day attention

They also have access to your full medical history and can coordinate care across multiple specialists if needed.

What I do differently

My work focuses on the patterns and root causes that often don't get addressed in a 15-minute appointment. I have longer visits, look at labs through a different lens, and use tools like acupuncture and herbal medicine that your PCP likely doesn't offer.

I'm especially helpful for:

  • Chronic symptoms that haven't responded to conventional treatment
  • Hormonal shifts and perimenopause support
  • Digestive issues, fatigue, and sleep problems
  • Stress and nervous system regulation
  • Preventive care and health optimization

But I'm not set up to handle urgent care, and I don't have hospital privileges or the ability to order imaging or refer to specialists within a health system.

Why you need both

Think of it this way: your PCP is the generalist who can handle a wide range of medical needs quickly. I'm the specialist who goes deep on the things that benefit from more time and a different approach.

Having both means you're covered for acute needs AND getting the kind of thorough, root-cause care that helps you feel your best long-term.

What to tell your PCP

I encourage you to let your PCP know you're working with me. You can share:

  • That you're seeing a naturopathic doctor for integrative support
  • Any supplements or herbs you're taking (I can provide a list)
  • Any labs I've ordered, so they have the results in your chart

Most PCPs appreciate knowing what else is going on with your care, and it helps them make better decisions if something comes up.

If you don't have a PCP

If you're currently without a primary care provider, I'd encourage you to establish one. It doesn't have to be complicated. You just need someone you can call when you're sick or need a referral.

If you're in the midcoast Maine area and need suggestions, I'm happy to share names of providers I trust.
